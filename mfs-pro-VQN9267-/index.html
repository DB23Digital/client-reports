What does all this mean? Plain-English glossary · 8 terms
Impressions
How many times your site showed up in a Google results list. Nobody has to click for this to count -- it just means Google put you on the page.
Like the number of people who walked past your shop window. Useful to know, but nobody has come inside yet.
Clicks
How many people actually clicked through to your site from those results.
The ones who opened the door and walked in.
CTR (click-through rate)
Of the people who saw you, the share who clicked. 2 clicks out of 100 sightings is 2%. A low number usually means the wording of your Google listing isn't tempting enough.
Of everyone who walked past, the share who came in. If hundreds pass and nobody enters, the window display is the thing to change.
Average position
Where you sit in the list of results, on average. 1 is the top of page one; about 10 is the bottom of page one; 30 is page three, where almost nobody looks.
Where you are in the queue of shops on the street. Being tenth still puts you on the street; being thirtieth puts you three streets away.
Striking-distance pages
Pages sitting between position 4 and 15 that already get real traffic -- close to the top but not there yet. These are the cheapest wins, because Google already likes them.
The exam questions you are getting 60% on. Cheaper to push those to 80% than to start a subject from nothing.
Top-20 position
The average position of your twenty busiest pages. We watch this instead of your whole-site average, because the whole-site number barely budges and can even look worse when you start ranking for brand-new searches.
The form of your first team rather than the whole club. It moves when the players who actually play improve.
GA4 sessions
Actual visits from Google, counted by Google Analytics. Clicks and sessions never match exactly -- the two tools count slightly different things.
Counted at your door rather than at Google's. The two rarely match exactly, and a big gap between them usually means the counter at the door needs checking.
Up and down arrows
Change since the previous check. For position, down is good -- moving from 8 to 5 means climbing. We colour it green when it's an improvement, whichever direction the number went.
Read them as a direction of travel, not a scoreline — each check covers Google's previous 28 days, so consecutive checks share most of their days.
